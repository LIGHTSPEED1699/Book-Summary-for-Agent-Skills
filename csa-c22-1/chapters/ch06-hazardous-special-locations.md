# Chapter 6 — Hazardous and Special Locations (Sections 18, 20, 22, 24)

## Overview
Section 18 classifies explosive atmospheres by Zone and selects equipment per Tables 18/18A. Section 20 applies it to fuel-handling facilities (service stations, propane, CNG, garages, bulk plants, finishing processes, aircraft hangars). Section 22 covers moisture/corrosion categories, sewage treatment plants, and livestock buildings. Section 24 governs patient care areas — bonding, isolated power, and essential electrical systems.

## Core Content

### Section 18 — Explosive atmospheres (pages 176–186)
- Classification (18-004): explosive **gas** atmospheres (18-006) → Zone 0 (continuous), Zone 1 (likely), Zone 2 (unlikely/short); explosive **dust** atmospheres (18-008) → Zone 20/21/22. Gas groups (IIC, IIB+H2, IIB, IIA; dust IIIC/IIIB/IIIA) and EPL terminology in 18-002.
- Equipment selection per **Table 18 (pages 465–467)**: Zone 0 needs EPL Ga (ia, ma, da…); Zone 1 adds Gb types (ib, e, p, m, q, o, d…); Zone 2 adds Gc types plus Division-2-rated equipment; temperature class per 18-054.
- Division equivalence per **Table 18A (page 468)**: IIC↔Groups A,B,C,D; IIB+H2↔B,C,D; IIB↔C,D; IIA↔D; IIIC↔Class II E; IIIB↔F,G; IIIA↔Class III.
- Per-zone rules: Zone 0 — 18-090 equipment, 18-092 wiring, 18-094 sealing. Zone 1 — 18-100 equipment, 18-102 wiring, 18-104 sealing, 18-106 motors, 18-108 luminaires, 18-110 flexible cords. Zone 2 — 18-150/152/154/156/158. Zone 20 — 18-190/192/194/196. Zone 21 — 18-200/202/204. Zone 22 — 18-250/254.
- General requirements: marking 18-050; rooms/areas 18-056/058; metal-covered cable 18-060; optical fibre 18-062; pressurized equipment/rooms 18-064; intrinsically safe/non-incendive 18-066; cable trays in dust 18-068; combustible gas detection 18-070; **seals 18-072** (cross horizontal AND vertical zone boundaries per 18-104/154); **bonding 18-074**; uninsulated exposed parts 18-076.

### Section 22 — Corrosive, wet, sewage, livestock (pages 198–203)
- Category definitions 22-002, applied per 22-004; only essential equipment in severe areas (22-100); equipment/luminaires/wiring devices per 22-102–108.
- Wiring methods: Category 1 → 22-200; Category 2 → 22-202; rinks → 22-204.
- Moisture/corrosion control: drip loops 22-300; drainage, sealing, exclusion of moisture 22-302; circuit control 22-400; corrosion-resistant materials 22-500; exposed non-current-carrying metal 22-600.
- Sewage treatment plants (22-700s): area classification 22-704 (Appendix B), wiring 22-706, equipment 22-708, grounding of structural steel 22-710.
- Livestock/poultry (22-800s): classification 22-804; equipment, luminaires, receptacles 22-806; wiring methods 22-808.

### Section 24 — Patient care areas (pages 204–210)
- Scope 24-000 (health care facility patient care areas); terminology 24-002.
- Basic care areas: circuits 24-104, bonding 24-106, receptacles 24-108, other equipment 24-110.
- Intermediate and critical care: circuits 24-112, bonding 24-114, receptacles 24-116; receptacles subject to standing fluids or drenching 24-118.
- Isolated systems (24-200s): sources of supply 24-202; single-phase isolated circuits 24-204; individually isolated branch circuits 24-206; three-phase isolated systems 24-208.
- Essential electrical systems (24-300s): circuits 24-302; transfer switches 24-304; emergency electrical power supply 24-306 = generator set(s) per CSA Z32, on the facility premises, in a service room/enclosure per CSA C282.

## Key Takeaways
- Zone classification drives everything: pick protection type (Table 18), convert Division↔Zone groups (Table 18A), then apply the zone's equipment/wiring/sealing rules.
- Seals (18-072, 18-104/154) cross horizontal AND vertical zone boundaries — the most-missed inspection item.
- Section 20 subsections are parallel: classify → Section 18 wiring → seal → disconnect → bond.
- Section 24 scales with patient risk: bonding/receptacle rules tighten from basic to critical care; wet/critical areas push toward isolated power; life-critical loads need essential systems (CSA Z32 generators, CSA C282 rooms).
