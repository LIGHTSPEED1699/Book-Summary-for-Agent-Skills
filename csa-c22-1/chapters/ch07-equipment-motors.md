# Chapter 7 — Electrical Equipment & Motors (Sections 26, 28)

## Overview
Section 26 governs installation of electrical equipment: switchgear, isolating switches, panelboards, capacitors, transformers, fences and vaults, SPDs, receptacles, appliances, electric heating, submersible pumps, and data-processing equipment. Section 28 covers motor installations: wiring, overcurrent and overload protection, control circuits, disconnecting means, refrigerant motor-compressors, and multi-winding motors.

## Section 26 — Installation of electrical equipment

### Switching and overcurrent devices
- **26-100**: isolating switches — marked "ISOLATING SWITCH" where required; **26-120**: indoor breaker rules; **26-140/26-142**: fuse installation.
- **26-420**: SPD connections (outdoor SPDs ≥12.5 mm from combustibles at service head).

### Capacitors (26-200s)
- **26-208**: conductor ampacity ≥135% of capacitor rated current (taps ≤7.5 m, ≥1/3 branch ampacity); **26-210**: O/C in each ungrounded conductor, ≤250% of rated current; **26-212**: disconnect within sight and ≤9 m (or lockable open) with 5 min discharge warning; **26-214**: disconnect rated ≥135% of rated current.

### Transformers, fences, vaults (26-240s to 26-360s)
- **26-300 to 26-324**: fences around equipment enclosures (also cited by outdoor floodlighting Rule 30-1016).
- **26-350**: no storage in vaults; **26-354**: vault construction per the National Building Code; **26-356**: illumination + grounding-type receptacle. **26-360 to 26-368**: film vaults.

### Panelboards
- **26-600**: prohibited locations — coal bins, clothes closets, bathrooms, stairways; dwelling units: handles ≤1.7 m above floor.
- **26-602**: a panelboard is required in every dwelling unit (exceptions: hotels/motels, non-metered subdivisions); single supply point, protected and disconnectable.

### Branch circuits
- **26-654**: dedicated circuits — refrigerator, laundry, utility, microwave cupboard, central vacuum, and (via **26-720 n**) EVSE per Rule 86-306.
- **26-656**: dwelling panelboard isolation; smoke/CO alarm interconnection per Rule 32-200 permitted with required label.

### Receptacles (26-700s)
- **26-702**: bonding of receptacles (Appendix B).
- **26-704**: Class A GFCI — receptacles within 1.5 m of sinks, bathtubs, showers; outdoor receptacles within 2.5 m of grade.
- **26-706**: tamper-resistant receptacles — child care, hotels/motels, preschools/elementary schools, dwelling units (exempt: above 2 m or otherwise inaccessible).
- **26-708**: wet-location receptacles need "Extra Duty" weatherproof covers (5-15R, 5-20R, 5-20RA, 6-15R, 6-20R, 6-20RA).
- **26-722**: dwelling placement — no point along usable wall line >1.8 m from a receptacle; kitchen counters: no point >900 mm; islands/peninsulas ≥600 mm × 300 mm get one (5-15R split or 5-20R); hallway: every point within 4.5 m.
- **26-724**: single dwellings — ≥2 outdoor duplex receptacles on opposite (or separated adjacent) sides at grade; ≥1 if perimeter ≤20 m; 1 per garage/carport car space; 1 within 1 m of each overhead door opener.
- **26-720**: required receptacle counts, incl. public corridors (1 duplex per 10 m) and EVSE car spaces.

### Appliances (26-740s)
- **26-740**: location of non-portable appliances; **26-742**: built-in cooking appliance tap conductors ≤7.5 m, ampacity ≥1/3 of branch O/C.
- **26-744**: single supply point; range receptacle 14-50R (≤50 A demand, 40 A min branch, ≤130 mm above floor); dryer 14-30R (>1500 W at 115 V, ≤30 A).

### Other equipment
- **26-800s**: electric heating; **26-950**: submersible pumps; **26-1000**: data-processing rooms — dedicated branch circuits.

## Section 28 — Motors

### Wiring methods
- **28-100/28-102/28-104**: conductor selection — ampacity per Table 37, 75 °C column basis; 90 °C conductors permitted for Class A motors.

### Overcurrent and overload
- **28-200s**: branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection.
- **28-300**: overload protection required (exceptions per 28-308, e.g. ≤1 hp manually started attended motors); **28-302**: separate current-responsive or integral devices; fuses must be time-delay type (Rule 14-200); **28-304**: fuses — one per ungrounded conductor, other devices per Table 25, three-phase motors need three current-responsive elements; **28-306**: trip ≤125% FLC (service factor ≥1.15) or ≤115% otherwise.

### Control and disconnecting
- **28-400**: undervoltage protection/release.
- **28-500 series** (28-502, 28-504, 28-506): control circuit wiring and overcurrent.
- **28-600s** (28-600, 28-602, 28-604): disconnecting means within sight of and ≤9 m from the motor and controller; alternatively lockable open.

### Specific motor types
- **28-700s / 28-710 to 28-714**: refrigerant motor-compressors (hermetic — rated current per marked rating).
- **28-800s (28-800 to 28-810/812)**: multi-winding and part-winding-start motors — protection sized per winding/starting method.

## Key Takeaways
- Dwelling receptacle layout is rule-driven: 1.8 m wall rule, 900 mm kitchen counter rule, hallway 4.5 m rule, outdoor/garage minimums (26-722, 26-724).
- GFCI (26-704) and Extra Duty covers (26-708) are location-triggered; tamper-resistance (26-706) is occupancy-triggered.
- Motor disconnecting means: within sight and ≤9 m, or lockable — mirrors Section 14 general disconnect principles.
- Section 28 overload rules hinge on Table 25/37 selections and the ≤125% FLC trip limit.
