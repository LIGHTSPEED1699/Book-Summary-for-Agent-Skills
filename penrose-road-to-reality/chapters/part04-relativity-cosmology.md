# Part IV: Relativity and Cosmology (Ch 28-33 — black holes and the second law, the big bang, the second law, conventions of the universe, quantum mechanics and the universe, geometry of the universe)

## Core Idea
Gravity is the entropy story of the universe. Black holes teach that gravity carries entropy (Bekenstein-Hawking S = A/4) and that the second law is the deepest law physics has; the puzzle is that the big bang began in an absurdly low-entropy state — Penrose's diagnosis: the Weyl curvature was (nearly) zero, and the "Weyl curvature hypothesis" is a law about initial conditions, not dynamics. The rest of the part questions the conventions we mistake for physics (units, time's arrow, the measurement postulates) and ends with the geometry of the universe: inflation's problems, and the search for a quantum-gravity theory that explains the arrow rather than assuming it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Black holes and the second law (Ch28)**: event horizons; Schwarzschild/Kerr geometry; singularities and cosmic censorship (naked singularities forbidden?); the four laws of black-hole mechanics; Hawking radiation and black-hole entropy S = A/4G; information-loss tension; inflation as a proposed (insufficient) entropy explanation.
- **The big bang (Ch29)**: Friedmann models (K = 0, ±1); expansion, redshift, CMB; big-bang nucleosynthesis; inflation's mechanism and Penrose's objections (it doesn't produce the low-entropy initial state, it assumes a special inflaton); conformal cyclic cosmology seed (the remote future's conformal rescaling).
- **The second law (Ch30)**: entropy as coarse-graining; Loschmidt's reversibility objection; why the initial state must be special; the Weyl curvature hypothesis: gravitational entropy is measured by Weyl curvature (zero at the bang, large at singularities/black holes); the arrow of time as an initial-condition law.
- **Conventions of the universe (Ch31)**: the "standard model's" unexplained parameters (masses, couplings, θ_QCD, the cosmological constant); units and dimensionless constants; the numbers of the universe (large-number ratios) demanding explanation.
- **Quantum mechanics and the universe (Ch32)**: the measurement problem at cosmological scale (no external observer!); many-worlds, Copenhagen, and Penrose's gravitational objective reduction (OR): a superposition of mass distributions has an ill-defined spacetime geometry; lifetime τ ≈ ℏ/E_G where E_G is the gravitational self-energy difference; OR as the physical R-process; experimental prospects (Kaseberg-type/matter-wave interferometry).
- **Geometry of the universe (Ch33)**: 3-geometry and twistors (preview of Part V); the conformal structure of spacetime as primary; twistor space as the arena where quantum gravity may be simple; the "three-complex geometry" program (ℂ, H, O division-algebra layers of spacetime).

## Key Concepts
- Bekenstein-Hawking entropy S = A/4 (in Planck units): entropy lives on horizons, not in volumes — the holographic seed.
- The second law is time-asymmetric only because the initial state was: the past hypothesis, made geometric via Weyl curvature.
- Weyl vs Ricci curvature: Ricci responds to matter (Einstein equations), Weyl is the free gravitational field (tidal fields, waves) — and its vanishing at the bang is the low-entropy clue.
- Cosmic censorship: singularities hidden behind horizons keep predictability; naked singularities would break it.
- Inflation's critique: exponential expansion smooths but requires a special inflaton state; it shifts, not solves, the entropy problem.
- OR criterion: τ ≈ ℏ/E_G — superpositions collapse when the gravitational self-energy of the difference reaches ℏ/τ; a testable modification of quantum mechanics.
- The measurement problem is unavoidable in cosmology: the universe has no outside observer, so R must be a physical process inside the universe.
- Dimensionless constants (α, mass ratios, Λ) are the real targets of a deeper theory; units are convention.

## Mental Models
- "Gravity is where entropy hides": matter thermalizes easily; gravity clumps — entropy counting must count geometry (horizon area).
- "The arrow is an initial condition": dynamics is time-symmetric; the past hypothesis (Weyl = 0 at the bang) does all the work.
- "Horizons have temperature": the four laws of black-hole mechanics are thermodynamics — the road to holography.
- "Collapse is spacetime indecision": a superposition of mass distributions = superposition of geometries; when the difference's self-energy is large enough, nature chooses (OR).
- "Question your units": only dimensionless numbers are physics; the universe's constants are a to-explain list.
- "Conformal structure first": angles/light-cones before scales — the CMB's low Weyl curvature and CCC's remote future both read the conformal story.

## Anti-patterns
- **Counting entropy with matter only** — the early universe looked "smooth" but that smoothness (Weyl = 0) is the extreme specialness; clumped = high gravitational entropy.
- **Treating inflation as the entropy explanation** — Penrose's objection: it needs a special inflaton and doesn't suppress Weyl curvature generically.
- **Assuming the measurement problem can be deferred in cosmology** — the universe's wavefunction has no external measurer; interpretations without a physical R strain here.
- **Confusing coordinate singularities with real ones** — the Schwarzschild r = 2M horizon is a coordinate artifact; the r = 0 curvature singularity is not.
- **Reading OR as many-worlds or Copenhagen** — OR is a third way: a real, gravitationally driven, testable collapse, not an interpretation.

## Key Takeaways
Black holes force entropy = horizon area/4: gravity's entropy is geometric, and the second law is the deepest physical law.
The big bang's low entropy is the central puzzle; the Weyl curvature hypothesis names it geometrically.
Inflation smooths but does not explain the special initial state — it assumes what must be explained.
Quantum measurement in a closed universe demands a physical collapse process; Penrose's gravitational OR (τ ≈ ℏ/E_G) is testable.
The universe's dimensionless constants and conformal geometry are the clues a deeper theory must explain.
