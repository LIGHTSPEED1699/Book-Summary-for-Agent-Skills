# Patterns — The Road to Reality (Penrose)

Recurring reasoning moves across the book.

## 1. Geometry-first
Re-express a physical law as a geometric fact: gravity = curvature, EM/strong/weak = gauge curvature, conservation = symmetry (Noether), spin = the topology of rotations. **Use when:** a physical equation appears — find the connection, curvature, or symmetry it is secretly naming.

## 2. The square-root move
Go to the double cover / square root to linearize: spinors for rotations (SU(2)→SO(3)), twistors for light rays, "field = √(geometry)". **Use when:** a rotation/orthogonal structure resists — pass to spinors or twistors where it becomes linear.

## 3. d² = 0 and its accounting
Build physics on exterior calculus: fields as forms, dynamics as dα = source, conservation from d² = 0, and global obstructions measured by cohomology (monopoles, Aharonov-Bohm, anomalies). **Use when:** a "potential exists locally but not globally" situation — that's cohomology, and it's physical.

## 4. Signature is destiny
Read the metric's sign convention to derive kinematics: light cones, proper time, E = mc², rapidity addition, the graviton's spin-2. **Use when:** relativistic reasoning — decide the causal/conformal structure before any computation.

## 5. Symmetry ⟹ conservation (Noether)
Every continuous symmetry yields a conserved quantity; every gauge redundancy constrains the interaction. **Use when:** building or checking a theory — inventory its symmetries and their charges.

## 6. Entropy as counting + initial condition
Second-law puzzles resolve by (a) counting the right degrees of freedom (gravity's entropy = horizon area) and (b) positing a special initial state (Weyl = 0). **Use when:** an arrow-of-time or thermodynamic-paradox question — locate the past hypothesis doing the work.

## 7. The audit: beauty AND refutation
Trust mathematical beauty as a guide (Dirac/Maxwell/Einstein track record) but demand experimental exposure; unfalsifiable elegance (string landscape) fails the audit. **Use when:** evaluating a theory or your own model — score both columns.

## 8. Necessity arguments (no-choice proofs)
Derive structures from consistency: complex amplitudes forced by QM, antiparticles forced by QM+SR, spin-2 forced by universal coupling, gauge fields forced by local symmetry. **Use when:** asking "why this and not something else?" — find the inconsistency that forbids alternatives.

## 9. The measurement crack
Treat the U/R split as a real physical problem, not interpretation: any complete theory must say when/why R happens (Penrose: gravity causes it, τ ≈ ℏ/E_G). **Use when:** quantum foundations appear — don't let "shut up and calculate" close the question.

## 10. Derived spacetime
Treat spacetime points as secondary; build them from more primitive incidence (twistors = light rays; spin networks; noncommutative/algebraic data). **Use when:** quantum-gravity reasoning — the continuum is the suspect, not the field on it.

## 11. Three-worlds bookkeeping
Track which world a claim lives in (physical/mental/mathematical) and respect the mysterious overlaps; Platonism as working faith, not proof. **Use when:** meta/philosophical drift — keep the ontology honest.

## 12. Constant-hunting
Treat dimensionless constants (α, mass ratios, Λ, θ_QCD) as data to be explained, not parameters to fit. **Use when:** a "successful" theory has free numbers — that's a symptom, not a virtue.
