# Puig & Simani Vol 1 (Ch1–6) — Fault Detection & Diagnosis: model-based, structural, set-based, stochastic, data-driven, AI

**Diagnosis and Fault-Tolerant Control 1: Fault Detection and Diagnosis** (ISTE/Wiley 2021, eds. Vicenç Puig & Silvio Simani). Volume 1 of a two-volume set; Vol 2 covers FTC (nonlinear, LPV, fuzzy/NN, MPC, virtual sensors). This file covers the Introduction + Ch1–6.

## The book's spine (Introduction, Puig & Simani)
- **IFAC SAFEPROCESS terminology** (the field's vocabulary is inconsistent; these are the agreed definitions):
  - **Fault** = unpermitted deviation of at least one characteristic property/parameter from acceptable condition. **Failure** = permanent interruption of ability to perform required function. **Malfunction** = intermittent irregularity. **Error** = deviation between measured/computed output and true value. **Disturbance** = unknown uncontrolled input. **Residual** = fault indicator from deviation between measurements and model-based computations. **Symptom** = change of an observable quantity from normal behavior.
  - **Analytical redundancy** = determining a variable in more than one way, at least one via a mathematical model (no extra hardware — software on the control computer).
  - **Diagnostic model** = relationships linking symptoms (inputs) to faults (outputs).
- **System properties**: reliability (perform under stated conditions over time), safety (no danger), availability (probability of satisfactory operation at any time).
- **Fault time-signatures**: abrupt = step (bias), incipient = ramp (drift), intermittent = impulse trains. Incipient faults hide under disturbances but flag replace-before-failure.
- **Fault location topology**: actuators, process components, **input sensors vs output sensors** (the distinction matters), controllers. Additive faults (summation on variables) vs multiplicative faults (parameter products).
- **The robustness problem is THE problem**: no perfect model; parameters drift; linear models of nonlinear plants. FDI must be insensitive to modeling uncertainty while sensitive to faults. Disturbances and faults act identically on the system — discrimination requires structure (disturbance distribution matrix) or robust design.
- Three classical residual-generation families (Patton, Basseville-Nikiforov, Gertler, Chen-Patton): **output observers/estimators, parity equations, identification/parameter estimation**.
- Open unsolved problems the book targets: reliable model estimation, modeling real uncertainty when unknown, estimating disturbance structure.

## Ch1 — Model-based FDI techniques (Simani)
- **Two-block architecture**: residual generation + residual evaluation (decision rule). Most research is on generation; evaluation is "straightforward if residuals are good" — which is exactly the robustness catch.
- **Fault modeling**: plant with u*(t), y*(t) true signals, sensors add faults, controller provides uR(t). FDI = observation problem on u, y.
- **Parameter-estimation residuals**: **Equation Error (EE)** (prediction error, LS/RLS solvable in real time) vs **Output Error (OE)** (simulation error, needs numerical optimization). Parameters Θ map to physical coefficients p (stiffness, damping, resistance) via invertible f(p) — Δp directly diagnoses the fault. Detection alone doesn't need the inversion.
- **Observer-based**: Luenberger/Kalman-Bucy; only output estimation needed, not full state. Variants: single observer excited by one output; **bank of observers** (one per fault hypothesis, Willsky's hypothesis test); **fault detection filters** (directional residuals — each fault drives the residual into a distinct direction/plane; isolation = nearest signature direction); **unknown-input observers (UIO)** — residuals decoupled from states, known inputs, and unknown inputs, sensitive only to faults (eigenstructure assignment, Chen-Patton).
- **Parity equations**: check consistency of measurements; parity vector for static/parallel redundancy first (Potter-Suman), then dynamic parity space; parity-space vs parameter-estimation comparison for additive/multiplicative faults (Höfling-Pfeufer).
- **Residual evaluation**: threshold on instantaneous or moving-average residuals; fixed threshold Δr_tol = ε·(normal std) — ε trades detection vs false alarms; statistical change detection: mean/variance estimation, likelihood-ratio/Bayes decision, run-sum test; fuzzy/adaptive thresholds; **symptom-vector direction + distance** in q-dim residual space for isolation (geometrical distance, probabilistic, NN, fuzzy clustering).
- **Robustification**: H∞ FDI (factorization approach Ding-Frank; H∞ filtering formulation: minimize disturbance effect / maximize fault effect; performance index inf‖r/f‖ sup‖r/w‖ over w-transformed frequency range); **active robustness** (model modeling errors as approximate disturbances, then decouple) vs **passive robustness** (adaptive threshold ε(t) generated by a linear system driven by u(t) — threshold tracks operating point, Clark 1989, Emami-Naeini 1988).
- **Neuro-fuzzy FDI**: NN as model or as classifier (noise-tolerant, generalizes, black box); fuzzy gives linguistic/interpretable models; NF hybrids: fuzzified NN inputs/interconnections, NN-generated membership functions; TSK + NN learning; NF residual generation = parallel models of fault-free and each faulty class; structure identification (Evolutionary Clustering, simplification) for transparency.

## Ch2 — Structural analysis (Krysander & Frisk)
- Model as **structure graph** (bipartite: equations E, variables X). **Matching** Γ; **redundant relation (RR)** from maximal matching; eliminating unknowns → **analytical redundant relation (ARR)** = residual generator. DAE models, derivative orders, reduced structure graph.
- **Dulmage-Mendelsohn decomposition** splits the model into underdetermined (G−), exactly determined (G0), and **structurally overdetermined (G+/M+)** parts. **Structural detectability**: fault f detectable iff its fault equation ef ∈ M+ (fault affects the overdetermined part).
- **Isolability**: fi isolable from fj iff Of \ Ofj ≠ ∅ (some observation consistent with fi, inconsistent with fj); structurally: efj ∈ (M \ {efi})+. Detection = isolation from the no-fault mode. **Fault isolability matrix** via one DM decomposition per fault. **Canonical isolability decomposition** of M+ into equation sets Mi where faults in the same block are mutually non-isolable.
- **Testable submodels**: PSO set (M = M+), **MSO set** (minimal PSO; structural redundancy degree 1; residual-space dimension = degree of redundancy). MSO/PSO = test equation supports (TES); test support = fault set of a PSO set. Algorithms: Krysander's minimal-overconstrained-subset algorithm; test **selection** needed (exponentially many tests; hitting-set and residual-selection literature).
- Sensor placement drives diagnosability (Krysander 2008). Tooling: Fault Diagnosis Toolbox (faultdiagnosistoolbox.github.io), LINKÖPING structural-analysis scripts.
- This is the bridge between the AI/consistency tradition (conflicts) and analytic redundancy (ARRs): same structural object, two communities.

## Ch3 — Set-based / set-membership FDI (Ye Wang & Puig)
- **Unknown-but-bounded** uncertainty (deterministic alternative to stochastic): w(k)∈〈0,Dw〉, v(k)∈〈0,Dv〉, x(0)∈〈c(0),H(0)〉; **zonotopes** 〈c,H〉 = center + generator matrix (symmetric, closed under linear maps + Minkowski sum, low complexity vs polytopes/ellipsoids).
- **Set-membership state estimation**: predict Xp(k)=A X(k−1)+Bu+Dw⊙bounds; intersect with output-consistent set Xy(k); X(k)=Xp∩Xy (Alamo et al.; DC programming). **Zonotopic observers** (interval observer, set-based observer Combastel): propagate estimated-output zonotopes directly.
- **Gain design by LMI**: robustness condition (Prop 3.4/Cor 3.1: contraction γ of P-radius ⇒ bounded steady-state size V(X∞)≤ε/(1−γ); gain G=P⁻¹Y); **zonotopic Kalman gain** (Prop 3.5, Combastel: minimizes Frobenius radius of H, independent of weighting W); **fault sensitivity condition** (Prop 3.6: H− index — residual center crf responds to sensor faults while robust to w,v).
- **FD decision**: residual zonotope R(k)=〈cr,Hr〉; alarm when measured output leaves the predicted interval set (Algorithm 3.4). Quadruple-tank example: robustness-only vs robustness+sensitivity designs compared on closed-loop data.
- Practical reading: zonotopic residual bounds = **tight, operating-point-dependent thresholds with formal guarantees** — the set-based cousin of Ch1's adaptive thresholds.

## Ch4 — Stochastic diagnosis (Provan)
- **Diagnosis as stochastic inverse problem**: forward model y=ξ(φ,η); infer fault/mode variable φ from observations y. Ill-posed + computationally heavy (thousands of forward evaluations).
- **Three-way taxonomy**: **model-driven** (invert first-principles model: Bayesian inversion π(φ|y)∝π(y|φ)π_pr(φ), MCMC, particle filters, Kalman/UIO-FDI as pseudo-inverse + statistical test), **data-driven** (learn pseudo-inverse classifier ξ⁻¹_Θ from data: ML classifiers, PCA T²/SPE plots, statistical methods), **hybrid/gray-box** (surrogates: data-driven tuning of low-fidelity models; data-driven augmentation of equations; multi-fidelity).
- Uncertainty representation: additive noise vs intrinsic (likelihood π(y|φ)); Bayesian formulation as PDF over modes; sequential filtering for φ_{1:k}.
- Diagnosis ≠ state estimation (that's P(x_k|Y_k)); diagnosis infers the mode/parameter that explains the anomaly.
- Comparative verdict: model-driven = interpretable, extensible to isolation, model libraries reusable; data-driven = no manual modeling but needs data covering fault distributions; hybrid = the promising middle (simpler than model-driven, data-hungry less than data-driven).

## Ch5 — Data-driven identification for FDI (Simani)
- **Errors-in-variables (EIV)** framing: both u and y corrupted (ũ, ỹ white zero-mean) — the realistic low-SNR case. High SNR → **EE identification** (ARX/MISO models, LS); low SNR → **Frisch scheme** (1934): from noisy I/O data, recover the noiseless generating model + noise variances via the data covariance matrix Σ*: null-space multiplicity (k−n) for k>n reveals the order; (r+1) limiting solutions in noise space. Dynamic Frisch (Beghelli), MIMO extension. EE models → Luenberger observers; Frisch models → Kalman filters.
- **Nonlinear identification for FDI**: hybrid/piecewise-affine (PWA) models — local affine submodels + switching/interpolation; universal approximation in Sobolev norm; identification under additive noise via EIV + clustering; local affine estimation, multiple-model estimation.
- **Takagi-Sugeno (TS) fuzzy models**: rules with affine consequents (hyperplanes); affine/homogeneous TS; = multiple-model structure (Leontaritis-Billings). **Product-space fuzzy clustering identification** pipeline: data collection → structure selection → **fuzzy c-means / Gustafson-Kessel clustering** (optimal cluster number) → local affine consequents + antecedent MFs → validation. TS prototypes as the fuzzy multiple-model for fault diagnosis.
- Why it matters for FTC later: TS/PWA models are the polytopic/LPV handles Vol 2 exploits.

## Ch6 — The AI approach to model-based diagnosis (Pulido, Alonso-González, Bregon)
- **KBS**: symbolic knowledge + inference engine + working memory; production rules (IF antecedent THEN consequent); forward/backward chaining; MYCIN (heuristic classification: abstraction → matching → refinement; CF certainty factors). Limits: device-dependent, knowledge-acquisition bottleneck, no first-principles explanation.
- **Consistency-based diagnosis (CBD, Reiter 1987)**: system (SD, COMPS, OBS); SD models **correct behavior only** (¬AB(c) ⇒ behavior); diagnosis Δ ⊆ COMPS s.t. SD∪OBS∪{AB on Δ, ¬AB elsewhere} satisfiable; **minimal diagnoses** under the minimal-diagnosis hypothesis; **conflicts** (AB-clauses entailed by SD∪OBS: at least one member abnormal); **Reiter's theorem: minimal diagnoses = minimal hitting sets of minimal conflicts**. Full-adder worked example (X1,X2 conflicts).
- **Dynamic extension — Possible Conflicts (Pulido & Alonso-González)**: for DAE models, hypergraph of equations+variables; **Minimal Evaluable Chains (MEC)**; a MEC with a valid causal assignment = **Minimal Evaluable Model (MEM) = Possible Conflict** — an executable monitor of a subsystem. **PCs ≡ minimal conflicts ≡ minimal ARRs ≡ MSO sets** (the equivalence that unifies AI and FDI communities). Three-tank system: 5 MECs, 3 yield PCs; DBCwithPCs software.
- AI vs FDI differences: AI focuses isolation/identification with qualitative component-level compositional models; FDI focuses robustness on system-level quantitative models; CBD has no exoneration (only rejection of inconsistent modes); no general dynamic extension of Reiter's characterization — temporal CBD (GDE, DISCORDER, TRANSCEND, DBC).

## Practitioner takeaways
- Pick the residual engine by what you have: accurate model → UIO/parity/H∞; noisy I/O both corrupted → Frisch/EIV; unknown nonlinear → TS/PWA clustering; no model → data-driven classifier; complex structured plant → structural analysis first (what's detectable/isolable at all before designing anything).
- Do **structural analysis before controller-style design**: DM decomposition tells you detectability, isolability matrix, and MSO test candidates cheaply; anything you design can't beat that ceiling.
- Robustness is a threshold question in disguise: fixed → adaptive (passive) → set-invariant (zonotopic) — increasing formality of the same idea.
- Conflicts/ARRs/MSO/PCs are one object; hitting-set computation is the isolation algorithm shared by both communities.
