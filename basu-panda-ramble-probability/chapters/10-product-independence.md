# Ch10 (Part III) — Product spaces, independence, infinite dimensions

Part III's motto (quoted in the book): "Infinite product spaces are the natural habitat of probability theory."

## Finite products
- **Product σ-algebra** ⊗Fᵢ = σ(rectangles ∏Aᵢ); on ℝᵐ, B(ℝᵐ) = ⊗B(ℝ) (generated by rectangles OR by open balls — same thing).
- **Monotone Class Theorem (10.3.3 ★)**: a λ/monotone-system argument to promote statements from "true on rectangles" to "true on all product-measurable sets" — the book's second inheritance engine (after good-sets in Ch4). Use it whenever a proof is "obvious for rectangles, check the class is closed".
- **Product measure (10.4.1)** and **General Fubini (10.5.1 ★)**: (µ×ν)((x,y)) iterated integrals; **Tonelli** (nonnegative — always swap) then **Fubini** (integrable — swap freely); n-factor version 10.6.2. Swapping is never free: check nonnegativity or integrability first.
- **Independent r.v.s**: σ(X₁),σ(X₂),… independent; equivalent (for countably generated targets) to P(X∈B₁, Y∈B₂) = P(X∈B₁)P(Y∈B₂); densities multiply: joint density = product of marginals. Pairwise vs mutual independence distinction again.
- **Multivariate normal (10.8)**: density via covariance matrix; uncorrelated jointly-normal ⇒ independent (special privilege of the Gaussian).
- **Convolution (10.9)**: X+Y density = ∫f(x−y)g(y)dy; sums of gammas/exponentials; the measure-theoretic statement: µ_{X+Y} = µ_X * µ_Y.
- Change of variables in ℝⁿ (10.12 ✖): Jacobian determinant rule, e.g. (X,Y)→(X+Y, X−Y).

## Infinite products — the payoff chapter
- **Cylinder sets** (10.10): specify finitely many coordinates, leave the rest free. Generalized rectangles ∏Aᵢ (all coordinates constrained) are **useless**: a countable product of proper subsets has measure 0 or is not measurable — "constructing σ-algebras using generalized rectangles leads to undesirable properties". Cylinders = finite-dimensional footholds.
- Product σ-algebra on ∏Ωᵢ = σ(measurable cylinders); measurable cylinders with rectangular faces form an **elementary family** → algebra → premeasure by products of probabilities → Carathéodory extension. **Thm 10.10.2 ★**: countable products of probability spaces exist with the product σ-algebra and the product measure (marginals preserved).
- **Kolmogorov Consistency Theorem (10.11.2 ★)**: given a consistent family of finite-dimensional distributions (marginalization-compatible), there exists a unique probability measure on the infinite product with those f.d.d.s. **This is how stochastic processes are defined**: specify the joint laws of every finite set of times consistently, and the process exists. (Processes section 10.11 ✖: random walks, Markov chains as measures on sequence spaces.)

## Practitioner takeaways
- Modeling a sequence of trials = Ch10.10, full stop. Any simulator, MDP trajectory space, or i.i.d. sample space is the product measure whose cylinder probabilities are products.
- Independence is a statement about σ-algebras, not variables' values — check it on generating π-systems (intervals), not all events.
- Consistency of finite-dimensional marginals is the *only* input needed to define a process; when debugging a "process" spec, hunt for inconsistent f.d.d.s.
