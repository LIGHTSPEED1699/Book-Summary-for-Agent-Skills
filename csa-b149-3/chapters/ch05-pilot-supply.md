# Chapter 5 — Pilot Supply (Clause 5)

## Overview
Pilots are the first flame in every ignition sequence; their fuel supply geometry determines whether they light reliably and safely. Clause 5 is short but strict: takeoff location and pressure adequacy.

## Core Content
- **5.1 Same fuel source as main train**: When the pilot taps the same fuel as the main valve train, the takeoff shall be:
  - a) **downstream of the appliance manual shut-off valve** (so one valve isolates everything);
  - b) from the **top or side of a horizontal supply pipe**, or the **side of a vertical supply** (avoids drawing condensate/debris);
  - c) at a point delivering **adequate pressure for stable pilot ignition**.
- **5.2 Alternate fuel source**: When the pilot uses a different fuel (e.g., propane pilot on a natural-gas burner), the takeoff still follows 5.1 b) and c) — top/side location and pressure adequacy. (No requirement to be downstream of the main manual valve, since it's a separate supply.)

## Why These Rules
- *Top/side takeoff*: keeps liquid, scale, and pipe dope out of the pilot orifice — the smallest restriction in the train.
- *Downstream of manual shut-off*: a single valve closure kills pilot and main fuel together for lockout/tagout.
- *Adequate pressure*: pilot stability under all upstream conditions; combined with Clause 4's independent pilot regulator.

## Quick Reference — Pilot Supply Checklist
| Check | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Takeoff point | Downstream of appliance manual shut-off (same-fuel case) |
| Pipe position | Top/side (horizontal) or side (vertical) |
| Pressure | Stable pilot ignition under all conditions |
| Regulation | Independent pilot regulator (Clause 4.2) |
| Manual valve | Dedicated pilot manual shut-off upstream of all pilot-train components (Clause 6.3) |

## Key Takeaways
- Pilot takeoff from the top or side — never the bottom — of the supply line.
- Same-fuel pilot taps go downstream of the appliance manual shut-off valve.
- Alternate-fuel pilots still obey position and pressure rules.
- The pilot train is independently valved and regulated end to end.
