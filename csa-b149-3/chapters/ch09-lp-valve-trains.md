# Chapter 9 — LP Gas Valve Trains (Clause 9)

## Overview
LP gas (propane/butane) in liquid-withdrawal or high-pressure vapour service punishes ordinary iron. Clause 9 upgrades materials and fittings for LP valve trains to handle higher pressures, oil contact, and vibration.

## Core Content
- **Pipe**: Minimum **Schedule 80** for LP valve train piping (vs Schedule 40 common elsewhere).
- **Fittings**: **Class 300** fittings where threaded/socket-weld construction is used (vs Class 150 standard).
- **Flanges**: **Class 150** flanges minimum.
- **Components**: All valve-train components shall be **certified/approved for LP gas service** — elastomers and materials compatible with LP (LP can wash lubricants and attack incompatible elastomers).
- **Rationale**: LP vapour trains often see higher inlet pressures than natural gas service (tank pressure can exceed 100 psig); heavier wall and higher class fittings resist stress, vibration, and mechanical damage typical of LP installations (cylinders, trucks, mobile equipment).
- Coordination: LP storage and handling (tanks, cylinders, relief valves) per **CSA B149.2**; the valve train requirements here start at the appliance side connection.

## Quick Reference
| Element | LP requirement | Typical natural-gas counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| Pipe schedule | Schedule 80 min | Schedule 40 common |
| Threaded fittings | Class 300 | Class 150 |
| Flanges | Class 150 min | Class 125/150 |
| Components | LP-certified | Gas-certified (may not cover LP) |

## Key Takeaways
- Schedule 80 pipe and Class 300 fittings are the LP valve-train baseline.
- Every component must be certified for LP service — a natural-gas-certified train is not automatically compliant.
- LP storage/handling lives in B149.2; this clause governs the appliance-side assembly.
- Vibration and road/transport applications (see Annex J food trucks) make material upgrades non-negotiable.
