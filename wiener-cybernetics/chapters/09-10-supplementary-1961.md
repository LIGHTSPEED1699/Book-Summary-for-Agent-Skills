# Ch9–10 — Supplementary Chapters (1961): Learning & Self-Reproducing Machines · Brain Waves & Self-Organization

## Ch9 — Learning machines
- **Ontogenetic learning** (individual, from experience) vs **phylogenetic learning** (racial, via reproduction + variation + selection); both are environmental adjustment; birds/insects lean phylogenetic (flight preempts the nervous system), mammals/man lean ontogenetic.
- Game-playing machines: von Neumann minimax assumes a perfectly wise opponent → **too cautious in practice**. Napoleon vs hidebound Austrians; Nelson vs continental fleets: victory came from exploiting the **statistically known opponent**, not from playing the perfect game. Chess books are the same: empirical valuations (pieces, mobility, command, development), not minimax.
- **Learning = second-order programming**: play with fixed valuations; periodically re-examine all recorded games and re-fit the valuation weights to maximize winning; then play as a new machine. Tricks that worked stop working. Samuel's checkers machine **beats its programmer after 10–20 operating hours**; Watanabe's extensions: prove a geometry theorem "elegantly" as a game against an idealized opponent ("Colonel Bogey"); logical induction as parameter fitting under economy/directness criteria.
- Stage-dependence: chess valuations must be fitted **separately per stage** (opening/middle/endgame) — master-level chess learning blocked by this (homogeneous checkers pieces = easier).
- The predictor analogy: first-order = linear filter on the immediate past; second-order = **non-linear** statistical determination of the filter coefficients from the long past (Wiener–Hopf coefficients determined non-linearly). **"A learning machine operates by non-linear feedback."**
- Mongoose vs cobra (Kipling's Rikki-Tikki-Tavi, Wiener's own observation): the mongoose's feints come earlier and earlier in phase relative to the cobra's strikes — the cobra acts per-stroke, the mongoose uses a segment of the fight's past → it fights like a learning machine. Same structure: roadrunner vs rattler, bullfight, small-sword duel, championship tennis (force the opponent into progressively worse returns).
- **The danger, stated 1961**: "learning machines cannot endanger us because we can turn them off" is false — turning off requires knowing the danger point, which the machine's speed outruns; the checker machine already beats its programmer. The fables are the ethics: Sorcerer's Apprentice (no stop-word), the genie in the bottle, **The Monkey's Paw** (literal-minded boons). "A learning machine must be programmed by experience… the values of winning employed in the war games must be the values we hold at heart… If we ask for victory and do not know what we mean by it, we shall find the ghost knocking at our door."

## Ch9 — Self-reproducing machines
- Two questions: combinatorial possibility (**von Neumann: yes**) and an operative procedure (Wiener: for the general class of **non-linear transducers** — single input function → single output function, determined by the past, time-translation invariant).
- Canonical form (Wiener, and independently **Gabor**): represent any non-linear transducer as a sum of fixed non-linear elements with adjustable linear coefficients; coefficients by least squares over the input ensemble → theory of **orthogonal developments**; concrete basis: **Hermite polynomials in the Laguerre coefficients of the input's past**.
- Trick that makes it practical: use **shot noise (tube noise)** as a universal input — its fluctuations approximate any curve, and it is **metric transitive / ergodic**: time average = ensemble average, so coefficients are measured by feeding the same noise to the unknown black box and the white-box replica, multiplying outputs, and time-averaging (add/multiply/average circuits suffice).
- Result: a **white box that can assume the characteristics of any non-linear transducer**, drawn into similitude of a black box automatically, no human intervention. Wiener's philosophical claim: this is "philosophically very similar" to a gene templating new genes from an indifferent magma of amino and nucleic acids.

## Ch10 — Brain waves as a self-organizing system
- Instrument history: electrophysiology waited on the vacuum tube (amplify without draining the nerve) and the cathode-ray oscillograph (electron beam as the lightest armature). EEG then rotted into pattern-recognition art because ink records defied mathematics.
- Fix: **FM magnetic tape** (erasure-immune) + autocorrelation hardware: two playback heads at adjustable spacing → f(t) and f(t+τ); square-law rectifiers + RC averaging → C(τ). Michelson interferometer = the same autocorrelation machine (fringe intensity ∝ autocorrelation; the most accurate spectrometer known — same math as EEG analysis).
- **Heterodyning** for hand computation: multiply C(t) by cos/sin 20πt to shift the 10 Hz band to zero, low-pass (average over ≥1/20 s), cosine-analyze the first, sine-analyze the second, add/subtract → right/left half-spectra. For near-sinusoidal autocorrelations: sample at 1/40 s with alternating signs and average.
- Findings: alpha spectrum peak within ~⅓ cycle; **sharp power drop at 9.05 Hz**; peak shape stable across days for the same subject — objective, reproducible quantities EEG had never had. Sampling-error theory (Brownian model in function space, Birkhoff ergodic theorem): RMS error of the approximate sampled spectrum ≈ sqrt(2B/A) relative units (B = autocorrelation cutoff 20 s, A = record 2700 s).
- **Why a clock? Gating.** Computing/control machines use clocks to gate: messages must be combined while still on the machine; synapses combine inputs only within a short fusion interval → need simultaneous arrival → gating. Lindsley's reaction times: delay = constant part + two parts uniform over ~1/10 s (CNS samples input every 1/10 s, output every 1/10 s) — gating at the alpha period, "very probably not fortuitous."
- **Driving the rhythm**: visual flicker near 10 Hz entrains the alpha rhythm; a German room with electrified floor/ceiling (electrical "flicker" by induction) felt equally disturbing. Frequency pulling ⇒ the mechanism is **non-linear** (linear systems can't shift frequencies).
- Model: the brain contains many ~10 Hz oscillators whose frequencies **attract** one another → power clumps into peaks and vacates gaps (the 9.05 Hz drop). Evidence: Rosenblith's **after-discharge** (a flash rings the cortex at ~10 Hz before dying out); the 23.5-h circadian rhythm pulled to 24 h by the environment; proposed experiment: fireflies (emitter+receiver) synchronized by a flashing neon tube, records analyzed by autocorrelation.
- Non-living parallel: **AC generators on parallel busbars** — fast generators take more load, slow ones less → frequency attraction → a "virtual governor" more accurate than any individual governor; the historical failure of series-connected alternators (frequency repulsion → instability) read as natural selection favoring the better-homeostasis parallel system.
- Generalization: **non-linear frequency attraction generates self-organizing systems** at any frequency — speculative extension to infrared molecular radiation patterns guiding gene/virus/cancer template reproduction; study TMV crystal absorption/emission spectra and irradiate the nutrient magma. (Flagged sub judice — a hypothesis, not a result.)

## Practitioner takeaways
- Learning = non-linear feedback on the parameters of the first-order controller; separate the fast loop (act) from the slow loop (fit the valuations), and fit per-stage.
- Program to your real values; machines grant what you asked for (Monkey's Paw rule for AI alignment).
- To identify a hidden clock/gating in any system, measure the distribution of its response latencies for uniform components at ~0.1 s scale.
- Coupled oscillators + non-linear coupling = self-organization (brain waves, power grids, fireflies, circadian entrainment): look for spectral clumps with adjacent gaps.
