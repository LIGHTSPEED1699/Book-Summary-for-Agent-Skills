# Ch1 — The Newtonian Program

## The argument
- Classical science's ideal: Laplace's demon — complete knowledge of the present + Newton's equations = complete prediction. This program is **false as a description of the real world**, not merely impractical.
- Even astronomy leaks: tides are dissipative, turbulent, lagged behind the moon — "oceanography is the meteorology of the hydrosphere." Not a single science conforms to the strict Newtonian pattern.
- The irreversibility revolution: Maxwell–Boltzmann–Gibbs reduced thermodynamics to statistics of ensembles; Planck/Bohr broke mechanism; Heisenberg's synthesis = statistical theory where present+past data predict the future **only statistically**.
- **Time's arrow is physical, not psychological**: birth is not the reverse of death; anabolism ≠ catabolism; "the individual is an arrow pointed through time." Bergson's durée gets a physical basis — but Wiener strips its mysticism: "Tyche is as relentless a mistress as Ananke" (chance is as deterministic-feeling as necessity).
- Consequence for method: where data are incomplete or systems are unstable, the **statistical description is not a concession — it is the correct theory**.

## Probability: statistics is the trunk, probability the branch
- Two camps: (a) probability as primitive, logical/subjective degree of belief (Keynes, Jeffreys — "plurality of probabilities" depending on the observer's knowledge); (b) **statistics as the primitive science of populations, probability defined as long-run frequency in a collective** (von Mises camp; Wiener's own position).
- Wiener's gambler's-fallacy test: any definition of probability must make the fallacy a fallacy. A system is "random" for statistical purposes when the relative frequency of an event in the sequence is stable and the gambler cannot beat it — **randomness is a property of the sequence, not of the event**.
- Roulette, cards, dice: mechanically deterministic, statistically tractable — determinism at the micro level does not purchase prediction at the macro level. The three-body problem: no analytic solution; instability makes long-horizon prediction physically meaningless.
- Practical maxim: decide whether your system is **predictable (dynamical), statistically predictable (ergodic-ish ensemble), or neither** before choosing your mathematics. Most sciences sit nearer meteorology than astronomy.

## Maxwell's demon (the seed of Ch3)
- A demon sorting fast/slow molecules seems to beat the second law. Resolution: the demon is coupled to the gas; the relevant entropy is gas+demon; **the information the demon receives is negative entropy** and must be paid for. This is the bridge from thermodynamics to communication theory.

## Fallacies & pitfalls
- "Perfect knowledge → perfect prediction" (Laplace's demon): false in principle — instability, dissipation, quantum statistics.
- Treating probability as a psychological primitive while doing physics: Wiener insists probability be grounded in verifiable frequency statistics.
- Assuming determinism where you cannot verify it buys anything: a deterministic system you cannot compute is, for every practical purpose, a random one.
